Bee’s Sound Garden: Free Printable Piano Improv Cards

Published September 18, 2026

Click to download the free PDF

Draw a card. Play the story on the piano. There are no wrong notes.

Bee’s Sound Garden is a deck of eighteen little stories from the garden, each one a shape for a student to play. A spider climbs her web, so the sound climbs. A leaf falls, so the sound falls. There is no right answer and nothing to check, which is exactly the point: it is the first improvising most beginners will ever do, and the card gives them somewhere to start.

How to play

  1. Shuffle the cards and set the deck face down on the music stand.
  2. Draw a card. Read the title, then the little line underneath. That line is the shape of the sound.
  3. Play the story. Any keys, as many as they like, for as long as they like. The only thing to follow is the shape.
  4. Play it again, differently. Louder, softer, faster, slower, one finger or a whole handful.

The only direction words on the cards are HIGH and LOW, printed in color so a pre-reader can find them. The eighteen cards are spread deliberately across the shapes a beginner can hear: sounds that rise, sounds that fall, sounds that stay down in the rumble, sounds that stay up in the sparkle, sounds that jump, and a few that need two voices at once.

Two decks in one download 🎨

The deck comes twice. The first is illustrated: every card has its creature drawn on it, ready to cut and play. The second is the same eighteen cards with a blank square where the picture goes, so the student draws their own spider, their own sleepy frog, their own fireflies. Drawing the card first and then playing it is often the part that unlocks a shy improviser.

You do not need both. Print the illustrated deck if you want a game today, print the color-your-own deck if you want a project, or print both and let the student choose which deck to draw from.

Printing and cutting

Print double-sided, flipped on the long edge. Each page of card backs is followed by its page of card fronts, so a duplex print lines them up. Print at 100% with no scaling, on card stock if you have it, and cut out the cards along the gaps.

The how-to-play sheet is page three, so print that one single-sided and keep it with the deck. The cards are standard poker size, the same as Find Every Key, so the two decks stack in the same box.

Why improvising, this early 🐝

Most first-year piano is about getting something right: the right key, the right name, the right count. That is most of what I make, and it matters. But a student who only ever plays for correctness never finds out that the piano is theirs to mess with.

These cards make that safe. The card decides the shape, so nobody has to face a blank keyboard, and there is no wrong answer inside the shape. A card takes thirty seconds, which means it fits at the end of a lesson, in the gap while you find the next book, or as the thing a student does while a sibling packs up.
